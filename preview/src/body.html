A quiet day and a missing day are not the same thing

Each cell is one aircraft on one UTC day. The archive this pipeline reads keeps only about 40 days and answers identically whether an aircraft did not fly or the day is no longer held, so the collector proves each day separately before recording it as quiet. Days it could not prove are excluded from every figure on this page rather than counted as zero.

Reading these numbers

What the pipeline can and cannot tell you

Flight time is airborne time

Departure is the last moment observed on the ground before the climb; arrival is the first moment observed back on it. That is wheels-up to wheels-down, so it is shorter than block time and not comparable to a published schedule.

Distance is a lower bound

Where coverage is lost, the aircraft is assumed to have flown straight, which it did not. The kilometres contributed by gaps are reported separately on every affected flight.

Some stops are inferred

Surface positions often are not received outside dense receiver coverage, so a turnaround can look like a hole in a cruise. Where a long gap is bracketed by low, slow fixes that barely moved, the pipeline splits the track — and labels both legs as estimates.

Costs are absent on purpose

No sourced operating figure for this fleet has been obtained, so no cost is shown. The engine is designed to publish an interval with a full breakdown and a citation for every input; until those exist it returns data unavailable rather than a plausible number.

Purpose is never inferred

Why a flight took place is entered by hand, with a publisher and a URL, and marked confirmed, probable or unknown. No political conclusion is generated from this data.

Everything is rebuildable

Raw observations are append-only. When the detection algorithm improves, every flight ever recorded is recomputed from the original data rather than re-downloaded — which matters, because the source archive has already deleted most of it.
